# 2026 Market Research Source Registry

**Registry date:** August 8, 2026  
**Scope:** Mobile application economics, current iOS/Android category leaders, web demand, subscription performance, and cross-platform product research.

## Purpose

This registry records the sources used to create the top-ten cross-platform category landscape and defines how each source may—and may not—be interpreted.

No single source answers all of the following:

- Which products are currently popular?
- Which products earn the most money?
- Which categories retain subscribers?
- Which web services have high demand?
- Which category offers a realistic entry opportunity?

The research therefore triangulates multiple source types.

---

## Source hierarchy

### Tier 1 — category economics and market direction

Use these for category-level spending, growth, conversion, retention, subscription, paid-acquisition, and platform economics.

#### Sensor Tower — State of Mobile 2026

- **Source:** https://sensortower.com/press/press-release-boosted-by-gen-ai-services-consumers-spent-more-money-in-apps-than-games-for-first-time
- **Used for:** 2025 global in-app-purchase spending; year-over-year growth; non-game versus game revenue; generative-AI and short-drama momentum; broad non-game revenue pools.
- **Strength:** Large commercial mobile-intelligence provider with global category estimates.
- **Limitation:** Individual app and market values are estimates based on Sensor Tower methodology, not audited store/company financial statements.

#### RevenueCat — State of Subscription Apps 2026

- **Source:** https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps
- **Used for:** Download-to-trial, download-to-paid, trial-to-paid, revenue per install, retention, refund behavior, AI versus non-AI economics, category performance, App Store versus Google Play monetization, and web-revenue adoption.
- **Strength:** Large first-party operational dataset from applications using RevenueCat infrastructure across iOS, Android, and web.
- **Limitation:** The sample reflects RevenueCat customers and subscription applications. It does not represent all apps, advertising businesses, commerce marketplaces, banking, travel booking, games, or enterprise contracts.

#### AppsFlyer — State of Subscriptions for Marketers 2026

- **Source:** https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/subscription-trends-report/
- **Used for:** Paid-install behavior, short-drama and streaming growth, spend concentration, and trial/paid-conversion context.
- **Strength:** Large mobile-measurement and attribution dataset.
- **Limitation:** Focuses on measured marketing/subscription activity; definitions and coverage differ from Sensor Tower and RevenueCat.

## Tier 2 — current application leaders and trend signals

Use these for current product names, directional grossing/download ranks, and category composition. Do not treat a chart position as audited revenue or durable product-market fit.

### Appfigures — May 2026 global app rankings

- **Source:** https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/most-downloaded-highest-earning-apps-may-2026
- **Used for:** Estimated global top-download and highest-earning mobile applications; current evidence for ChatGPT, TikTok, YouTube, CapCut, Google One, and other leaders.
- **Strength:** Current app-intelligence estimates with a transparent distinction between downloads and revenue.
- **Limitation:** Revenue is estimated and generally represents mobile store economics after store fees. It does not capture all web, advertising, commerce, enterprise, or off-store revenue.

### AppBrain — current U.S. application rankings

- **Root:** https://www.appbrain.com/stats
- **Used for:** Directional snapshots of current top-grossing iOS and Google Play products by category in the United States.
- **Strength:** Makes category-level current charts available for both ecosystems.
- **Limitations:** Ranks can change daily; methodology and coverage should be treated as directional. Store-grossing ranks understate products monetized through advertising, commerce, bookings, payments, lending, brokerage, and enterprise contracts.

## Tier 3 — web demand and cross-platform behavior

Use these to identify major web services, discovery behavior, web-first competitors, and the role of web in a category. Web visits are demand signals—not proof of revenue, subscriptions, or profitability.

### Cloudflare Radar — 2025 Year in Review: Internet Services

- **Source:** https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2025-year-in-review-internet-services/
- **Used for:** Global relative rankings across generative AI, social, messaging, ecommerce, streaming, and financial services.
- **Strength:** Traffic-derived view across major Internet services and categories.
- **Limitation:** Relative traffic rank is not directly comparable with mobile downloads, active users, revenue, or profit.

### Similarweb — Digital 100 and category rankings

- **Source:** https://www.similarweb.com/corp/digital-100/
- **Used for:** Fast-growing digital properties, website traffic trends, and category-level web demand.
- **Strength:** Broad web/digital measurement and competitive comparison.
- **Limitation:** Public pages expose only part of the underlying dataset. Traffic estimates can differ from company-reported users and do not show product economics.

## Tier 4 — primary product and regulatory sources

Each category dossier should add primary sources specific to the products and risks being evaluated.

Examples include:

- Official App Store and Google Play product listings.
- Official pricing and terms pages.
- Company investor-relations and regulatory filings.
- Product documentation and security/privacy policies.
- Platform developer and review policies.
- Government regulator guidance.
- Public APIs and data-provider documentation.

Primary sources should override third-party summaries for product features, published pricing, policies, and disclosed company financials.

## Source-use rules

1. **Do not mix downloads, monthly active users, website visits, subscribers, gross billings, and revenue.** Label the measure precisely.
2. **Do not treat third-party revenue estimates as audited facts.** Use terms such as “estimated” and identify the provider.
3. **Record the observation date for volatile data.** App rankings, pricing, and product features can change quickly.
4. **Use country and platform qualifiers.** A U.S. iOS rank is not a global Android rank.
5. **Separate market proof from entry attractiveness.** A giant incumbent proves demand but may also prove the category is difficult to enter.
6. **Use multiple independent evidence types before ranking an opportunity.** Category economics, product leaders, user complaints, pricing, distribution, and build cost should agree.
7. **Prefer primary sources for legal, regulatory, policy, pricing, and technical claims.**
8. **Preserve contradictory evidence.** Do not discard a credible source merely because it weakens the desired idea.
9. **Do not use current store charts as a historical time series unless snapshots have been retained.**
10. **Re-check live sources before committing capital.** This registry captures the market as researched on August 8, 2026.

---

## Evidence confidence labels

Each future analytical claim should use one of these labels internally:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | Primary disclosure, regulator/platform documentation, or multiple consistent high-quality sources |
| **Medium** | Reputable third-party estimate or one strong source with understandable methodology |
| **Directional** | Current rank, traffic estimate, anecdotal review pattern, or incomplete public data |
| **Hypothesis** | Inference requiring customer or behavioral validation |

Opportunity scores are always analytical judgments, even when their input data are high-confidence.
